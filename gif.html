General rule: GIFs compress best when large pixel areas remain identical between frames and the overall color palette stays small and stable.

Pre-Processing Tips

  • Cut motion where possible. Static backgrounds and fewer moving regions compress better.
  • Simplify backgrounds. Flat color or blurred backgrounds compress better than detailed scenes.
  • Lower frame rate before export.
  • Reduce hue and saturation range where possible.
  • Limit color variety. Gradients, glow, noise, film grain, and subtle texture increase file size.
  • Avoid transparency where possible. Hard edges on solid backgrounds compress better than soft translucent edges.
  • Avoid flicker. Rapid brightness changes, flashing UI, and grainy animation increase complexity.
  • Hold on keyframes longer. Fewer unique in-between frames keep motion readable while reducing change.
  • Use clean edges and larger shapes. Tiny text, thin lines, and fine detail tend to shimmer and expand the palette.
  • Preprocess the source. Reduce noise, limit colors, and trim unnecessary motion before encoding.
  • Keep colors stable between frames. Color shifts or grading changes reduce compression efficiency.
  • Avoid subtle background motion. Moving shadows, gradients, or noise fields force more pixel changes.
  • Prefer clear shapes over smooth gradients. Gradients often introduce many similar colors.
  • Lock camera movement where possible. Even slight camera drift forces most pixels to change each frame.
  • Use solid fills instead of textures. Texture overlays introduce many small color variations.
  • Reduce dithering during preprocessing. Dither patterns introduce noise and increase frame differences.
